Yours

The items, their routes and their D-minus are from your master. The orders, the customers, the people and the five days of entries are made up for this week, so that every screen has something to show.

A stand-in

Every speed is a stand-in until your rates arrive. Where the plan says a day is over capacity or a step is late, that follows from the stand-in rate, not from your factory. The shape of the week is real; the numbers are placeholders.

How to read it

Your day first — one page per role, what you enter and what you read. Then the week, day by day. Then what is recorded and what is not, and the checks: what we expected, what the screen said, pass or not.

Your day

What each person at U&M does on the system, when, and on which screen — as it was done in this week. Nothing is entered twice: a figure typed once is what every other screen reads.

The HOD of a department

Morning · Production

Who came in and the overtime hours for the day, two boxes, one Save. The sentence beside them says what the day is running at and its man-hours — on day 1 Stitching read "10 of 12 in — the day is running at 83% of the rate · 80 man-hours. 1 of 7 rates have no crew size and are unaffected.". Handed to you, not yet counted in: what the department before you says it sent; enter what arrived, and a difference is kept.

Through the day · the floor display

The wall screen for the department: what is due today, what it is waiting on, who is in, which machines are running. It refreshes itself.

End of day · Production

What you were asked for: one row per job, the target beside it. Enter good and rejected separately. Under a row with rejects, why rejected: the cause and how many, as many causes as the day needs. Entering a day again corrects it. Then press nothing else: the evening run is PPC's.

HR

Morning · Manpower

Deployment: mark each person In, Out or On leave, and the overtime hours against anyone who stays. The department's head count and its hours follow at once, and so does what it can make that day. Who is in is the whole factory on one page: on the books, in, out, on leave, overtime, man-hours, and who has not been marked.

Maintenance

When a machine stops · Machines

Book downtime: the machine, breakdown or maintenance or changeover, from and to, a reason. The department's day is scaled by the machines running, the plan re-runs, and Attention carries a card until it is back. Remove the booking when it is.

Stores and purchase

When a count is done · Material

Click On hand against a material and type the count. The screen says what the plan needs against the store, and what is short and by when; a count that covers it moves the material out of the short list.

Merchandising

When an enquiry comes · Accept an order

Item, pieces, container date, Check. In a few seconds: Yes, or which departments cannot make it and why, with the whole factory planned around it and nothing saved. Add it to the book on a Yes; Take it anyway on a No, which asks for the reason and keeps it on the order, in the book and on Attention. A second container is added from the Order book.

PPC

Morning · Command centre, Attention, the heatmap

The plan as it stands: breaches, flagged days, the constraint department; every finding that needs an answer today, each linked to its screen; the days that are too full, department by department.

During the day · Schedule, What if, Capacity sheet

Drag a bar to hold a department's start where the floor needs it, with a reason: a pin every later run honours. What if tries a second shift, overtime or a department down without touching the live plan. The Capacity sheet is where the rates, the crew sizes and the D-minus live, and where your workbook loads.

Evening · Command centre

Run the schedule. The run keeps every day the departments have entered as it was planned, places what remains from the next working day, and says what moved.

The MD

Any time · Dashboard, WIP, Attention, Forecast, Money

Nine figures and the week as a grid, planned against made, department by day; every order's progress in units; what needs an answer today; how much of the forecast is worth believing, from the history it has; cash out, week by week. Read, not entered.
