The five things

  1. How many pieces a day each department makes of each item, and with how many people. This is the one figure everything else rests on. Today every department is planned at the same stand-in speed, so every date, every red square on the heatmap and every answer on Accept an order is a placeholder. With your figures the dates are yours.
    Form: the capacity workbook (KRAM/04). Blank workbook on the Capacity sheet hands it out already filled with your items and departments; one row per item, two boxes per department — pieces a day when the department is working on nothing else, and the crew that figure was measured with. Load it back on the same screen. Where a figure is not known, leave the box blank rather than guessing: a blank is honest and the screen says so.
  2. For each department: once it finishes a lot, how many days before the next department can start on it. Drying after finishing, inspection, moving the lot, the margin you keep before packing. About twenty numbers, one per department, the same whatever the item and however many pieces. With these and the speeds above, the system can work out the D-minus for every order itself — a 300-piece order and a 1,000-piece order of the same item get different dates, as you told us they should — instead of PPC keeping a sheet of day-counts per item.
    Form: a list, department by department, in days. Zero is a valid answer.
  3. Does Machining feed Ply Cutting? The route we hold says it does. Your sheet does not say either way: Machining's day-count sits beside a combined Ply cutting and assembly column. If Ply Cutting does not wait for Machining, one connection is removed on Masters and one breach on the sample plan goes with it.
    Form: yes or no, from whoever runs the wood shop.
  4. One real export from Panipuri. An order list as the ERP gives it, any week, any format. It decides how orders will reach Kram without being typed, and one question only the file can answer: does Panipuri hold the container stuffing date, or only the customer's delivery date?
    Form: the file itself (KRAM/05 asked for this on 17 August).
  5. For three or four items: what goes into them, and what they cost. The bill of materials and the article cost. The Material screen can then say what is short against the store, and the Money screen what the plan will spend, week by week. Three items are enough to show it working; the rest can follow.
    Form: however it exists today — a costing sheet, a BOM print, a photo of either.

Two smaller ones

  1. Who should see what. Kram has a screen for each role — the HOD, HR, maintenance, stores, merchandising, PPC, the MD. Today every role can read every screen. Tell us if that is wrong: should maintenance see the customer order book, should stores see commercial quantities?
    Form: a sentence or two. And the names and email addresses of the people who will use it, so their accounts can be made.
  2. The overtime ceiling. The shift settings hold a maximum of five overtime hours a person a day. If your compliance adviser's figure is different, the plan should use theirs.
    Form: a number of hours.

What happens when they arrive

The workbook loads in one go and the plan re-runs on your speeds the same day. The handover days take one change on our side, after which the D-minus is printed per order rather than typed. The route answer is one click. The Panipuri file is read once by us and then every week by the system. The three items' costs go onto the Capacity sheet and the Material and Money screens fill. Nothing you send is changed by us: a figure you gave is a figure the screen shows, and the screen says where each one came from.
