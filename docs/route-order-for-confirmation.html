One question, and it is the whole point of this note

Is this what actually waits for what?

Every line in the table above is our reading of the trade, not anything you told us. The ones we are least sure of are Foam Pasting — does it really wait for the finish to be dry, or does it start earlier? — and whether Fiber Processing and Cutting truly depend on nothing at all.

Anything missing in the other direction?

The table says a department waits for something. It is just as useful to know where we have said that and it is not true — two departments we have put in sequence that in practice run at the same time on different pieces.

Why this matters more than the order didTwo things it changes

Warnings. Kram holds each department back until everything feeding it is due. Wire a feeder into the wrong place and it will warn you that Metal Finishing cannot start because Wood Finishing is not done — which is not true, and not a problem overtime can fix. The warnings stop meaning anything, on a screen whose whole job is warnings.

Quantities. Losses compound backwards: a department has to make enough that the shipped quantity survives every loss after it. But only the losses on the path its own material takes. A wooden leg goes from Ply Cutting into the frame — it never enters Stitching, so it should not be made in larger numbers to cover Stitching's rejects. When the route was one line it was, by a wide margin. Getting this structure right is what stops the factory reading as busier than it is.

The position numbers you confirmed are still there, and still decide the order things are listed in. They no longer decide what waits for what — that is this table, and only this table.

One rule Kram will hold you to

A department's D-minus must be earlier than everything that feeds it. If Stitching feeds Stapling, Stitching has to be finished first — so its D-minus must be the larger number.

Kram checks this as you enter the sheet. If a D-minus contradicts the table above it says so, names both departments, and tells you whether it is already causing false warnings. It will not change anything on your behalf — which of the two figures is wrong is your call, not the software's.

Changing it

  1. Sign in and open Masters.
  2. Find What feeds what. It is a grid: read each row as “this department cannot start until…”, and the ticked columns are what it waits for.
  3. Click a cell to add or remove one. A row with nothing ticked is a starting point.
  4. Two departments cannot wait for each other — if you try, Kram refuses and tells you why.
  5. Nothing else needs doing. The schedule recalculates as soon as you change it, and the effect is visible on the next screen you open.

What we need back

The corrected table — or a note against the lines that are wrong. If it is easier to mark up a printout than to open the screen, that is completely fine; send it back however suits you.

Until then Kram is running on our version of it, which is a guess made carefully rather than a fact. Everything downstream of it — the deadlines, the load on each department, which department is the bottleneck — is only as right as this one table.
